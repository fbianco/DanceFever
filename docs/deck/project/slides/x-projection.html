A bone of length ℓ tilted by φ out of the picture plane looks ℓ·cos φ long (subject far away: ΔZ ≪ Z).

With landmark noise σ, its direction wobbles by δa ≈ √2·σ / (ℓ·cos φ), which diverges as φ → 90°: a bone pointing at the camera has no 2D direction.

So in Dance Fever: moves live in the picture plane (a sideways “flamingo” knee, not a forward knee lift), legs weigh less, and the network’s depth z is not used.

A test keeps moves apart: holding move i scores below 0.85 on move i + 1, for every consecutive pair in all three dances.

The camera is a projection from three dimensions to two. Seen from the side, a bone of length l tilted by phi out of the picture plane shows up with length l cos phi, for a subject far from the camera compared to its depth. That costs more than length: if each landmark has a positional noise sigma, the measured direction of the bone has an angular error of about root two sigma over l cos phi, which blows up as the bone points at the camera. A forearm pointing straight at the lens has no meaningful direction in the image. This is why the choreographies were designed in the picture plane: the knee lift is done sideways, as a flamingo, not forwards; why legs have lower weights; and why the game does not rely on the network’s estimated depth z, which is inferred from a single image and is much noisier than x and y. An automated test also checks that no two consecutive moves are so similar that holding one would score PERFECT on the next; it caught a real design bug during development.
