A PHOTO IS TINY DOTS

The webcam takes 30 photos every second.

Each photo is made of tiny coloured squares called pixels. This smiley has 144 of them.

For the computer, the pink cheek is just three numbers: 255 red, 79 green, 216 blue.

A webcam photo has about half a million pixels. The computer never sees you: it only sees numbers!

Start with what a computer actually gets from the webcam. Ask the children: what do you see when you look very, very closely at a screen? Tiny squares! Each square is called a pixel. The smiley on the slide is made of 144 of them. For the computer, a pixel is not a colour: it is three numbers saying how much red, green and blue light to mix. The white-outlined pink cheek is 255 red, 79 green and 216 blue. A webcam photo of 960 by 540 pixels is about half a million pixels, and the webcam sends 30 such photos every second. So the big question of the next slides is: how can a computer that only sees numbers find a dancing child in all of them?
