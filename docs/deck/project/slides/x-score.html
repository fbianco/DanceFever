STEP 4 · THE ARROWS

The score: cosines, clamped and averaged

cos θ_b = u_b · v_b v = the coach

s_b = clamp((70° − θ_b) / 50°, 0, 1)

S = Σ_b w_b·s_b / Σ_b w_b seen bones only

rating = max S(t), t ∈ [T − 0.2 s, T + 0.5 s]

Weights w: upper arm 1, forearm 0.8, torso 0.6, thigh 0.5, shin 0.4. Legs count only in moves that use them.

The window reaches further late than early: players see the move, then copy it. It never overlaps the next move’s window.

bone score s against angle θ

The comparison itself is elementary. For each bone, the cosine of the angle between the player’s direction u and the coach’s direction v is their dot product. The angle is mapped to a bone score that is 1 below 20 degrees, 0 beyond 70 degrees, and linear in between. The pose score S is the weighted mean over the bones the camera saw clearly; upper arms weigh most, shins least, because legs are often cut off by the frame and noisier. A move is judged on the best S inside a window from 0.2 seconds before its beat to 0.5 seconds after, because people react to what they see. On the chart, the dashed lines show the rating thresholds; for a single bone they are reached at 27.5, 35 and 45 degrees. The game’s tests check that a perfect copy of the coach gets five stars, a clumsy one about one star, and that holding the previous move never earns a PERFECT on the next.
