Two things make the tracking work in time. First, MediaPipe’s video mode: once a person is found, the crop for the next frame is predicted from the current landmarks, so the expensive detector is skipped; it only runs again when the landmark network’s presence score drops below the tracking threshold of 0.5. With two dancers the model returns up to two poses, and the game assigns them to player 1 and player 2 by the horizontal position of the shoulder centre, with a short memory so that a lone dancer keeps their seat when the other one steps out. Second, smoothing: each landmark goes through an exponential moving average with alpha 0.55. That is a first-order IIR low-pass filter: its impulse response decays geometrically, with a time constant of about 42 milliseconds at 30 frames per second. Its gain is 0.89 at 2 hertz, the speed of dance moves, crosses minus 3 decibels near 4 hertz, and is down to 0.43 at 10 hertz, where frame-to-frame jitter lives. Only 42 milliseconds of lag, which the judging window easily absorbs.
